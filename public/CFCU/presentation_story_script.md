# Modernizing the Member Journey

## Presentation script for Census Federal Credit Union

**Recommended length:** 12–15 minutes, followed by discussion  
**Core idea:** The website is not merely a digital brochure. It is the connective tissue between a member's first moment of interest and the action Census FCU wants them to take.

**The story thread:** Ask the audience to imagine one ordinary, unnamed member. They notice an auto-loan promotion while at work, return to it later on their phone, see the same clear message, apply without friction, and become part of a journey leadership can measure. This is an illustrative scenario, not a claim about an actual member.

---

## Before you begin

Do not memorize every word. Learn the first two sentences, the final question, and the transition at the end of each slide. Treat the rest as a set of beats you can say naturally.

When you advance a slide, pause for a second before speaking. Let the audience look first. On the visual slides, turn toward the screen briefly, point to one detail, and then return your attention to the room.

---

## Slide 1 — Modernizing the Member Journey

**Purpose:** Establish the central idea and invite the audience into a member's point of view.

**Say:**

“Before we talk about websites, I want to start with a person.

Imagine a Census employee who sees an auto-loan promotion during a busy workday. They are interested—but they have a meeting in five minutes. So they do what most of us would do: they make a mental note and keep moving.

Later, when they have a moment, they pull out their phone and look for Census Federal Credit Union. What happens next matters. Do they immediately recognize the offer? Can they find the rate? Can they tell what to do next? Can they act without having to call someone for help?

That short distance—from interest to action—is the member journey. Today, I want to show you how Census FCU can make that journey feel simple, connected, and worthy of the trust members already place in this institution.”

**Transition:** “I’ll walk through what I found, what the experience could become, and a practical way to build it.”

---

## Slide 2 — Today's Agenda

**Purpose:** Give the room confidence that the conversation has a clear destination; establish your personal connection briefly.

**Say:**

“We’ll begin with the opportunity in the current experience. Then I’ll show you a vision for the new site, the four parts of the member journey, the outcomes we should expect to measure, and the phased approach I’m proposing.

I also want to briefly explain why this project matters to me. I’ve spent more than 20 years creating digital experiences, including 14 years here at the Census Bureau. So I understand this environment, the people you serve, and the importance of earning trust. I’m not approaching this as someone who simply wants to make a site look newer. I’m approaching it as someone who wants the digital experience to serve the Census community as thoughtfully as the credit union itself does.”

**Transition:** “With that perspective, I reviewed the current site through a member’s eyes.”

---

## Slide 3 — The Opportunity

**Purpose:** Frame the gaps as solvable growth opportunities, not criticism.

**Say:**

“What I found was not a broken foundation. I found a good institution whose digital front door is not yet doing enough of the work for it.

Four opportunities stood out.

First, the mobile experience can remove friction from the very first click. Second, the website and the promotions members see elsewhere can speak with one voice. Third, useful, actively managed content can build credibility and give people reasons to return. And fourth, the path to joining or applying can be much clearer.

These are connected. If a member cannot comfortably use the site on a phone, the promotion loses momentum. If the message changes from one channel to another, trust weakens. If the path to action is unclear, interest disappears.

So the opportunity is not simply a redesign. It is to make every step reinforce the next one.”

**Transition:** “Let me make that concrete by showing you the kind of first impression we can create.”

---

## Slide 4 — The New Site

**Purpose:** Help the audience picture the future without getting trapped in design details.

**Say:**

“This is a concept, not a final design. What matters today is the hierarchy.

When a member arrives, the site should answer three questions almost immediately: Am I in the right place? What is most important right now? What can I do next?

Here, the strongest offer is visible. Rates, services, and applications are easy to reach. The navigation is calm rather than crowded. The site is doing what a good branch employee would do: welcoming the person, understanding why they came, and helping them take the next step.

Think back to our member from the opening. They should not have to remember the exact wording of the promotion or search through several pages. The website should recognize the intention they arrived with and meet it.”

**Optional audience prompt:** “When a member lands on the current site, what is the single action you most want to make easier for them?”

**Transition:** “That experience is built through four steps, and each step creates the conditions for the next.”

---

## Slide 5 — Four Pillars

**Purpose:** Give the audience a memorable framework.

**Say:**

“The plan is simple enough to remember in four words: Attract. Connect. Convert. Measure.

Attract means the experience is easy to find, easy to use, and credible—especially on a phone.

Connect means every channel carries the same message, whether a member sees it on a building screen outside the cafeteria, in an email, or on the website.

Convert means we remove unnecessary steps between interest and action.

Measure means we learn what members actually respond to and improve from evidence.

This is a loop, not four unrelated features. Better measurement helps us attract more effectively the next time. But it begins with the device already in the member’s hand.”

**Transition:** “So let’s return to that member and follow the journey.”

---

## Slide 6 — Attract

**Purpose:** Make mobile-first design feel like member service, not a technical preference.

**Say:**

“Our member is between meetings. They are not sitting at a desk ready to study a website. They have perhaps thirty seconds and one thumb.

A mobile-first experience respects that reality. The menu is simple. The featured rate is visible quickly. Apply, search, and contact are obvious actions. The page loads fast, the type is readable, and nothing requires pinching or hunting.

That may sound like design detail, but it is really service. We are saying, ‘We understand your time is limited, and we will not make you work to do business with us.’

The goal of this first step is not to tell the member everything. It is to give them confidence that they are in the right place and make the next action unmistakable.”

**Transition:** “But a good mobile experience only carries the journey so far. The message also has to feel familiar.”

---

## Slide 7 — Connect

**Purpose:** Show how coordinated channels preserve trust and momentum.

**Say:**

“This is where the journey becomes connected.

The member remembers the auto-loan promotion they saw at work. When they reach the website, the same offer is waiting for them. If they later receive an email, it uses the same language and leads directly to the same destination.

The member should experience one credit union—not a collection of separate screens, pages, emails, forms, and staff messages.

Consistency does two things. It builds confidence because the message is recognizable, and it reduces effort because the member does not have to start the search over every time they change channels.

Behind the scenes, that also gives Census FCU a more coordinated way to plan promotions and understand which messages are earning attention.”

**Transition:** “Once the member recognizes the offer, the next question is simple: how easy is it to say yes?”

---

## Slide 8 — Convert

**Purpose:** Define conversion in human terms and connect it to service.

**Say:**

“Conversion can sound like a marketing term. Here, it simply means helping a person finish what they came to do.

Our member lands on the site, finds the promotion in seconds, chooses ‘Apply,’ and sees a clear path forward. There are no unnecessary detours and no uncertainty about whether they clicked the right thing.

The same principle applies to joining the credit union, finding a form, or contacting the right person. Every extra step creates another chance for someone to postpone the decision or leave.

Larger financial institutions have trained people to expect clear, polished digital experiences. Census FCU does not need to become a giant institution. It needs to offer that same level of clarity while preserving the personal service that makes a credit union different.

That is the advantage: digital convenience without losing the human relationship.”

**Transition:** “And after the member acts, the journey should not disappear into a black box.”

---

## Slide 9 — Measure

**Purpose:** Reassure leadership that decisions and refinements will be evidence-based.

**Say:**

“Today, it can be difficult to know which promotion created interest, which page helped someone act, or where people gave up.

Measurement closes that gap. It lets leadership see what members visit, what they click, how many applications begin, how campaigns perform, and which promotions deserve continued investment.

This is not about collecting numbers for the sake of a dashboard. It is about answering practical questions: Did members understand the offer? Did they reach the application? Did one message work better than another? Where should we improve next month?

Our member’s journey—from seeing the promotion to beginning an application—now gives Census FCU something it can learn from. The next campaign becomes smarter because the last one produced evidence.”

**Transition:** “When those four steps work together, the value appears in very practical places.”

---

## Slide 10 — Practical Outcomes

**Purpose:** Translate the framework into organizational value without promising unsupported numbers.

**Say:**

“The payoff is not simply a more attractive homepage.

It is more people completing the membership journey. More interested borrowers reaching a loan application. Campaigns whose performance we can actually see. Fewer calls from members who only need help finding a routing number, rate, or form. And clearer monthly visibility for leadership.

There is also a less measurable—but equally important—outcome: the entire experience feels like one Census Federal Credit Union. The branch, the building screen, the inbox, and the website all reinforce the same relationship.

That gives staff a better tool, leadership better visibility, and members an easier experience.

The question is not whether a website can look more modern. The question is whether the digital experience can help the credit union serve and grow. These outcomes are how we will know that it does.”

**Transition:** “The safest way to reach that future is to build it in focused phases.”

---

## Slide 11 — The Ask

**Purpose:** Resolve the opening story and ask for a specific next step.

**Say:**

“We began with one member who noticed an offer during a busy day.

In the experience we have just walked through, that moment of interest is no longer lost. The member finds a fast, clear mobile site. They recognize the same message across channels. They reach the application without friction. And Census FCU can see enough of that journey to make the next experience even better.

The proposal delivers that vision in three phases.

Phase One establishes the foundation: a focused audit, the website redesign, and the mobile-first experience. Phase Two connects promotions and email campaigns. Phase Three adds the measurement and refinement that support continued growth.

We do not have to solve everything at once. We need to begin with the part that makes everything else possible.

So the decision I’m asking for today is this: **Are we aligned on moving into Phase One and defining the scope, timeline, and responsibilities needed to begin?**”

Then stop. Let the room answer. Do not soften the question by immediately continuing to speak.

---

## If the room is quiet after the ask

Wait three full seconds. Then use one of these prompts:

- “What would you need to feel comfortable taking that next step?”
- “Which part of Phase One would you like to examine more closely?”
- “Who else needs to be involved in shaping the scope?”

---

## Likely questions and concise answers

### “How long will this take?”

“The precise timeline should come out of Phase One scoping, once we confirm content ownership, approvals, integrations, and technical constraints. I’d rather give you a dependable schedule based on that discovery than an attractive date based on assumptions.”

### “What will you need from us?”

“A decision-maker, a day-to-day point of contact, access to the current platform and analytics, and timely review from the people responsible for compliance, products, and member communication.”

### “How will we know it worked?”

“We’ll agree on a small baseline before launch—such as mobile engagement, visits to priority products, application starts, campaign clicks, and common member-service requests—then report against it consistently.”

### “Are you proposing all three phases now?”

“I’m presenting the full vision so every phase has a purpose. The immediate commitment I’m asking for is Phase One, with the later phases shaped by what we learn and what Census FCU is ready to prioritize.”

### “Can this work with our current systems?”

“That is one of the first things the technical audit should confirm. The design should respect the systems, security requirements, approval process, and vendor constraints Census FCU already has.”

---

## Delivery guardrails

- Describe the member story as an illustrative scenario. Do not imply it is a documented member case.
- Avoid promising a specific increase in memberships or applications until a baseline and targets are agreed upon.
- When mentioning larger credit unions, compare the clarity members expect—not the size, feature set, or budget of those institutions.
- Treat the mockups as directional. The slide itself labels them conceptual, so reinforce that verbally.
- If pricing, procurement, compliance, hosting, integrations, or exact timing have not been settled, position them as items to define during Phase One scoping.

---

## One-minute version of the whole presentation

“A member may first encounter Census FCU on a building screen, in an email, or through a search—but the website is where interest either becomes action or disappears. I’m proposing a member journey built on four steps: attract people with a fast, mobile-first experience; connect every channel with one consistent message; convert interest through clear paths to join and apply; and measure what works so leadership can improve with evidence. We can deliver that in three phases, beginning with the audit and redesign that make everything else possible. The decision I’m asking for is whether we are aligned on moving into Phase One and defining the scope, timeline, and responsibilities to begin.”
